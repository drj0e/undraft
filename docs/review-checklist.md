# Pre-Publish Review Checklist

The standard the model reviewer (the daily "Blog pre-publish reviewer" routine) follows before stamping a post `reviewed: true`. It lives in the repo so the bar is version-controlled and auditable, the same way `review-log.md` is. The routine prompt should say: *"Follow docs/review-checklist.md. Log per the format there."*

Scope: future-dated, not-yet-live posts that lack `reviewed: true`. For each, work the checks below, then write one line to `docs/review-log.md`.

## Checks

1. **Self-citation fidelity (FAIL bar).** For every claim a post makes about what another post said, listed, or argued, open the cited post and verify the claim against it. This is not "is it roughly consistent" — it is "does it match."
   - Verify the **count and membership** of any enumeration. If the post recaps a list, it must have the same items the source listed. A recap that drops, adds, or substitutes a member is a **FAIL**, not an acceptable "simplification." Changing how complete a list looks changes what the reader concludes.
   - Watch for a **new idea framed as a forgotten one**: "I listed A, B, C and left one off." That is only faithful if the source listed exactly A, B, C as a closed set. If the source's list had other members, or never claimed to be complete, the framing is false even when each individual word checks out.
   - `scripts/lint_posts.py` prints two advisories that point you at the risky spots: "possible dropped list member" (a citing list that is a subset of the linked post's list) and "self-citations to verify." Treat every advisory line as a required check, not a suggestion. The script only surfaces; you decide.
   - **Worked example (the miss this check exists for):** *the-property-i-left-off-the-stack* recapped *the-stack-nobody-talks-about*'s "selectable, constrainable, auditable, affordable, and stoppable" as "selectable, constrained, audited, and stoppable" — silently dropping *affordable* — then presented "resumable" as the property it had "left off," though resumable was never on that list. The earlier review waved this through as "omits 'affordable' but does not contradict it, a simplification." That was the wrong call. Dropping a member to set up the hook is a FAIL.

2. **Substance recycling (FAIL bar).** Compare against the nearest prior post by theme. A fresh opening over a retread argument is still a retread. The post must carry one new load-bearing point the named neighbor does not make. Name that point in the log.

3. **External-source truth (FAIL bar).** Verify every statistic, named-entity claim, quote, and outbound link via WebFetch. An uncited number or a dead/misquoted link is a FAIL. (The deterministic gate already blocks leftover `[SOURCE NEEDED]`/`[JOE]` placeholders.)

4. **Feed clustering (NOTE only, never a hold).** Run `scripts/check_diversity.py`. Log a NOTE on an actionable clash, but do not hold — you cannot fix an adjacency with nothing to slot between.

5. **Rhetorical tics (fix-before-stamp bar).** Run `scripts/check_tics.py`. The deterministic gate already hard-fails the banned phrase forms (`TIC_KILL`); this covers the frequency moves a regex can't judge. If the post under review breaches a per-post budget, uses a move the trailing window has worn out, or extends a closer-shape streak (see CLAUDE.md "Rhetorical Tics — The Rotation List"), reword to break the pattern before stamping. Rewriting a flip or a closer is in the reviewer's scope; changing the argument is not. Log a NOTE naming the tic and the fix. If the post's whole structure is the tic — a flip in every section, a templated closer carrying the thesis — that is a **HELD** like any other failed bar. Flags on already-live posts are context, not work: published history does not get edited for style.

## Substance bar (checks 6-9)

These extend check 2 from "new against our own feed" to "new against the field, and true enough to survive it." They exist to **toss** posts, not polish them: a retread thesis or a naked load-bearing claim is not fixable by rewording, only by a different argument. Run them immediately after check 2 and before checks 4-5 — there is no point de-ticcing a post that dies here. Checks 6 and 7 are toss-level (HELD). Checks 8 and 9 are revise-level: fixable by a targeted edit, HELD only when the fix would have to be a different post. For every check below, log what you nominated, searched, or marked — silent N/A and silent favorable nomination are the leniency paths, and an unlogged pass doesn't count.

6. **Field novelty (FAIL bar, toss-level).** Take the post's one new load-bearing sentence (check 2 already produces it) and test it against the field. **Spend one search for prior art on that sentence and log the query** — declining to search is not a pass.
   - No nameable prior after the search: PASS.
   - A prior exists (a canonical study, a framework, folklore, a consultancy line — "verification is the bottleneck," "measure satisfaction, not adoption," "fix your data before you AI it," Fowler's Harvested Framework): the post passes only with a stated **operative delta** — a decision, distinction, or test it hands the reader that the named prior is silent on or would disagree with. Write one sentence stating what the prior fails to give you; if you cannot write that sentence, FAIL.
   - The prior must contain the post's *distinction* at comparable specificity, not merely share its topic. "Verification is the bottleneck" does not swallow "can a change stay the unit of account" — a slogan kills only if its standard reading already yields the post's prescription.
   - Dodges that are not a delta: a checklist restating the thesis as questions; a coined name for the held belief; re-aiming at a junior audience (the audience is always the well-read practitioner). **Auto-FAIL: the post's own cited sources assert its thesis.**

7. **Load-bearing assertions (FAIL bar, toss-level).** Enumerate **every** sentence containing a number, a frequency word ("most," "almost always," "the median case"), or a cost/effort comparison. For each, delete it and ask whether the post's closing prescription still follows. Every sentence whose deletion breaks the argument must be classified as exactly one of:
   - **(a) cited** to a checkable source;
   - **(b) witnessed** — grounded in a specific incident or artifact shown in this post, with checkable specifics. An incident grounds only the claims it witnessed: the counterfactual arm of a comparison cannot inherit (b) from the arm that was performed, and "I've done this before" or an anonymized vignette is not (b);
   - **(c) experience-scoped** — explicitly bounded to first-hand vantage ("every program I've watched," "in my shop") with the scope carried through the argument. FAILs only if the conclusion silently upgrades it to field-wide fact, and a scope never licenses fake precision: experience supports "most," not "north of 80%";
   - **(d) conceptual** — true by definition or by derivation shown in the post. Any sentence about how systems, teams, or markets typically behave is empirical regardless of register; phrasing it in a conceptual voice does not make it (d);
   - **(e) naked** — none of the above. **Any load-bearing sentence in class (e) is a FAIL.** Log the enumeration and each classification. The classes are closed; the definitions decide, not the post's register.

8. **Existence and scarcity claims (FAIL bar, revise-level).** Trigger: any claim that a product, tool category, or practice does not exist or is rare — "doesn't exist yet," "nobody does this," "almost no platform," and the hedged forms too: "rarely," "few teams," "most teams skip." Hedging the quantifier does not exempt. Only load-bearing instances count: if the argument survives the thing existing, "nobody" is color for the tic pass, not this check. For each trigger, **run one search for the strongest counterexample a hostile commenter would post** — not the weak one the post pre-named — and log the query and top result even on a pass. The counterexample must do the claimed-missing thing for the claimed audience: a product feature does not refute a claim about an organizational grant. If the post names it and the distinguishing clause survives sixty seconds on that product's landing page, PASS — verify the clause here, because check 3 only covers cited claims. If the post never engages it: **REVISE** — add the naming-and-distinguishing sentence yourself and log it. **HELD only if the counterexample defeats the thesis**: when the thing the post says is missing simply exists, no added sentence fixes that.

9. **Reference recital (FAIL bar, revise-level).** Mark every block (paragraph or list) that fails the **disagreement test**: the post's cited source, or an obvious standard reference, contains every claim in the block and would endorse every sentence. An embedded aphorism does not un-mark a block — strike the aphorism and re-test the remainder. Transformation means deriving something the source does not say (Part 11's audit-trail rules re-read as a spec sheet for an unattended agent); annotating what it does say is recital. Then the **subtraction test**: delete all marked blocks. If the residue still carries a post that passes checks 6 and 7, REVISE — compress the post toward the residue. If the residue cannot stand alone, HELD: the post is a summary of its bibliography with a voice on top.

**Worked examples (calibrate on all three — the dodges live in 6 and 9):**
- *The toss this bar exists for.* who-watches-the-watcher passes checks 1-5: links resolve, the incidents are real, nothing recycles our own feed. It dies here twice. Check 7: "they catch north of 80% of the problems agents introduce" — load-bearing, fake precision no experience scope can carry, class (e) as published. Check 8: "that pipeline between 'agent wrote code' and 'code is ready to merge' doesn't exist yet" — one search returns CodeRabbit, Greptile, Qodo, none engaged, and their existence defeats the framing. Verdict: HELD, not editable into a pass.
- *A check-6 dodge, rejected.* github-pages-not-aws: the thesis is the law-of-the-instrument genre piece every senior engineer pre-holds. The dodge: "certs-as-identity making tool choice harder is a new distinction." It renames the hammer and hands the reader no decision the genre doesn't already hand them. Not a delta; FAIL stands.
- *A check-9 dodge, rejected.* data-governance-that-survives-an-inspection: the role list and nine-step rollout are DMBOK restated; the embedded aphorisms ("...owners and no stewards is a press release") do not un-mark the blocks. Struck and re-tested, the blocks are recital. Subtraction leaves the regulation-as-budget argument, which stands alone on 6 and 7. Verdict: REVISE toward the residue, and the log line says which blocks were cut.

## Outcome

- All FAIL-bar checks pass → set `reviewed: true`; log `PASS` with a one-line rationale per dimension (facts / recycling / self-citation / substance / consistency), naming what you verified — including the check-6 search you ran and the check-7 classifications.
- Revise-level findings only (checks 5, 8, 9) → make the targeted edit yourself, then stamp; the PASS line names the edit. If the fix would change the argument rather than add a sentence or cut a block, it isn't revise-level — quarantine it.
- Any toss-level check fails (1-3, 6, 7; or 8 where the counterexample defeats the thesis; or 9 where no residue stands) → set `draft: true` (quarantine); log `HELD` with the reason. The HELD/NOTE line is what triggers the email alert.

Log format mirrors existing entries, e.g.:
`- 2026-06-26 the-property-i-left-off-the-stack: HELD, self-citation: recaps the-stack-nobody-talks-about's 5-item list as 4, drops "affordable", and frames "resumable" as a left-off member it never was.`
